The Vera C. Rubin Observatory began the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), a ten-year optical survey of the southern sky, on 30 June 2026. This paper presents early Rubin images and discoveries, focusing on the data management system that makes science at this scale possible. Building on  LHC-scale distributed computing experience, Rubin deploys HEP tools, including Rucio and FTS for data movement, CernVM-FS for software distribution and HTCondor for workflow management at its three data facilities: SLAC, an ATLAS Tier-2 site, and the French and UK facilities, both long-standing LHC Tier-1 centres. We describe the end-to-end Rubin computing ecosystem and lessons from this cross-disciplinary collaboration for  future data-intensive experiments.
